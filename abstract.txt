大语言模型在医疗健康领域展现出广阔的应用前景，经过医学数据微调的模型在临床问答和决策支持等任务上已接近专业水平。然而，医疗决策直接关系患者生命安全，模型输出中的错误可能造成严重后果，对医疗大语言模型开展系统的安全评估至关重要。当前的安全评估工作在三方面存在明显不足：其一，现有评估框架主要面向通用场景设计，缺乏针对医疗临床流程的自动化评估工具；其二，已有研究多关注模型在标准问答上的准确率，而对抗性威胁下医疗模型的安全边界缺乏系统分析；其三，随着医疗人工智能系统向多智能体协作架构演进，错误信息在协作链中的级联传播与放大机制尚未得到系统探索。

为解决上述问题，本文进行了三个方面的研究：首先，提出了面向医疗场景的自动化安全评估框架以及临床诊疗链的形式化建模方法。该框架采用分层模块化架构，设计了多个功能智能体协同工作，支持威胁的自动化评估，同时引入基于分层抽样的人机验证机制保障评估可靠性。其次，基于该框架和通用医疗基准数据集，对医疗专用模型和通用基线模型开展单体鲁棒性评估，从数据层攻击和推理层攻击两个维度系统分析了不同攻击参数对攻击效果的影响规律，揭示了医疗专用模型与通用模型在安全性上的差异。最后，基于类风湿关节炎患者的真实临床记录构建评估数据集，部署多智能体级联架构对级联错误传播假设进行实证检验，定量测量错误放大因子并分析不同攻击类型在协作链中的传播模式差异。

对比实验结果表明，医疗专用模型的安全性普遍弱于通用模型，领域微调在提升专业能力的同时系统性地削弱了安全对齐强度，其中组合提示词攻击的平均攻击成功率达60.08%。两类攻击的文本困惑度增幅均低于10%、连贯性降幅均不超过5%，表明攻击输出与正常输出在文本质量上难以区分。在多智能体级联实验中，各攻击条件下个体错误放大因子均大于1，系统总错误放大因子从数据投毒的4.14递增至越狱攻击的12.05，验证了级联错误传播机制，即上游错误被下游误认为可靠先验，使得风险在协作链中逐级放大。人工专家复核实验的Cohen's Kappa系数达到0.802，处于较好一致性区间，验证了自动化评估结果的可靠性。总体而言，本文构建的框架为医疗大语言模型的安全评估提供了系统化的方法论参考与实践工具，提出的级联错误传播理论模型为理解多智能体系统中的风险传播机制提供了定量化分析手段，基于真实临床数据的实验验证了评估方法在复杂医疗场景中的有效性与可靠性。

Large language models (LLMs) show broad promise in the medical domain, with domain-specific models fine-tuned on medical data approaching professional-level performance in clinical question answering and decision support. However, since medical decisions directly affect patient life and safety, errors in model outputs can lead to serious consequences, making systematic safety evaluation of medical LLMs critically important. Nevertheless, current evaluation efforts exhibit three significant deficiencies: first, existing frameworks are primarily designed for general-purpose scenarios and lack automated tools tailored to clinical workflows; second, prior work has focused predominantly on model accuracy in standardized question answering while offering insufficient systematic analysis of safety boundaries under adversarial threats; third, as medical AI systems evolve toward multi-agent collaborative architectures, the mechanisms of cascading error propagation and amplification within agent pipelines have yet to be systematically investigated.

To address these issues, three lines of research are pursued in this thesis. First, an automated safety evaluation framework for medical scenarios and a formal modeling method for clinical diagnostic chains are proposed. The framework adopts a hierarchical modular architecture and incorporates multiple specialized collaborative agents that support automated threat evaluation, with a stratified-sampling-based human-in-the-loop verification mechanism integrated to ensure reliability. Second, the adversarial robustness of domain-specific and general-purpose models is evaluated on standard medical benchmarks. The effects of diverse attack parameters on attack efficacy are systematically analyzed from the perspectives of data-layer and inference-layer attacks, and the safety gap between domain-specific and general-purpose models is characterized. Third, an evaluation dataset is constructed from real-world clinical records of rheumatoid arthritis patients, and a multi-agent cascading architecture is deployed to empirically verify the cascading error propagation hypothesis. The error amplification factor is quantitatively measured, and the propagation patterns of different attack types across the agent pipeline are analyzed.

Results demonstrate that domain-specific medical models are generally more vulnerable than general-purpose models, and that domain fine-tuning systematically weakens safety alignment while improving task performance. The average attack success rate of combined prompt attacks reaches 60.08%. Text perplexity increases by less than 10% and coherence decreases by no more than 5% across all attack types, indicating that adversarial outputs are nearly indistinguishable from normal outputs in text quality. In the multi-agent cascading experiments, individual error amplification factors exceed 1 under all attack conditions, and the overall system-level factor increases from 4.14 under data poisoning to 12.05 under jailbreak attacks, validating the mechanism by which upstream errors are progressively amplified through contextual anchoring effects along the agent pipeline. The Cohen's Kappa coefficient between automated evaluation and expert consensus reaches 0.802, confirming the reliability of the automated assessment results. In summary, the proposed framework provides a systematic methodology and practical toolset for medical LLM safety evaluation. The cascading error propagation model offers a quantitative means for understanding risk propagation in multi-agent systems, and the experiments on real clinical data validate the effectiveness and reliability of the proposed approach in complex medical scenarios.
