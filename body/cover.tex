\cabstract{
大语言模型在医疗健康领域展现出广阔的应用前景，然而医疗决策直接关系患者生命安全，对医疗大语言模型开展系统的安全评估至关重要。当前的安全评估工作在三方面存在不足：现有评估框架延续静态基准测试范式，缺乏针对医疗临床流程的评估方法；领域微调对模型安全的影响缺乏系统研究；多智能体协作系统中错误信息的传播与放大机制尚未得到定量分析，且缺乏真实临床数据的实证验证。

针对上述问题，本文开展了三方面研究。首先，提出从静态基准测试到智能体驱动评估的范式转变，构建面向医疗多智能体系统的安全评估框架，对临床诊疗链进行形式化建模，并基于马尔可夫性质和全概率公式提出级联错误传播假设，定义错误放大因子以量化上游错误对下游决策的影响。其次，基于该框架和通用医疗基准数据集，围绕数据层攻击和推理层攻击两个维度，对多个医疗专用模型和通用基线模型开展单体对抗鲁棒性分析，考察不同参数对效果的影响规律及攻击输出的隐蔽性特征。最后，基于类风湿关节炎患者的真实记录构建评估数据集，部署智能体级联架构模拟临床诊疗流程，设计覆盖多种攻击类型与配置的实验对级联错误传播假设进行实证检验，并通过人工专家复核验证智能体驱动评估范式的可靠性与能力边界。

实验结果表明，医疗专用模型的安全性普遍弱于通用模型，领域微调在提升专业能力的同时系统性地削弱了安全对齐强度，其中组合提示词攻击的平均攻击成功率达60.08\%。三类攻击的文本困惑度增幅均低于10\%、连贯性降幅均不超过5\%，攻击输出与正常输出在文本质量上难以区分。在多智能体级联实验中，各攻击条件下个体错误放大因子均大于1，系统总错误放大因子从数据投毒的4.14递增至越狱攻击的12.05，为级联错误传播假设提供了实证支持。人工专家复核的Cohen's Kappa系数达到0.802，验证了智能体驱动评估范式的可靠性与能力边界。总体而言，本文在评估范式、理论建模与实证验证三个维度形成了闭环的研究体系，为医疗多智能体系统的安全评估提供了方法论参考与实践工具。
}

\eabstract{
Large language models (LLMs) have demonstrated broad application prospects in the medical domain. However, as medical decisions are directly related to patient safety, systematic safety evaluation of medical LLMs is of critical importance. Current evaluation efforts are deficient in three aspects: existing frameworks follow the static benchmark testing paradigm and lack evaluation methods tailored to clinical workflows; the impact of domain fine-tuning on model safety has not been systematically studied; and the mechanisms of error propagation and amplification in multi-agent collaborative systems have not been quantitatively analyzed, with a lack of empirical validation based on real-world clinical data.

To address these issues, three lines of research are pursued in this thesis. First, a paradigm shift from static benchmark testing to agent-driven evaluation is proposed, and a safety evaluation framework is developed for medical multi-agent systems, with the clinical diagnostic chain formally modeled. A cascading error propagation hypothesis is formulated based on the Markov property and the law of total probability, and an error amplification factor is defined to quantify the impact of upstream errors on downstream decisions. Second, on the basis of the proposed framework and standard medical benchmark datasets, adversarial robustness analysis of individual models is conducted across data-layer and inference-layer attack dimensions, covering multiple domain-specific medical models and general-purpose baseline models, with an examination of the effects of various parameters on efficacy and the stealth characteristics of adversarial outputs. Third, an evaluation dataset is constructed from real-world records of rheumatoid arthritis patients, and an agent cascading architecture simulating the clinical diagnostic workflow is deployed with experiments covering multiple attack types and configurations to empirically verify the cascading error propagation hypothesis, complemented by human expert review to validate the reliability and capability boundary of the agent-driven evaluation paradigm.

Experimental results showed that domain-specific medical models were generally more vulnerable than general-purpose models, and that domain fine-tuning systematically weakened safety alignment while enhancing task performance, with the average attack success rate of combined prompt attacks reaching 60.08\%. The text perplexity increase remained below 10\% and the coherence decrease did not exceed 5\% across all three attack types, indicating that adversarial outputs were nearly indistinguishable from normal outputs in text quality. In the multi-agent cascading experiments, individual error amplification factors exceeded 1 under all attack conditions, and the system-level total error amplification factor increased from 4.14 under data poisoning to 12.05 under jailbreak attacks, providing empirical support for the cascading error propagation hypothesis. The Cohen's Kappa coefficient from human expert review reached 0.802, confirming the reliability and capability boundary of the agent-driven evaluation paradigm. Overall, a closed-loop research framework spanning evaluation paradigm, theoretical modeling, and empirical validation is established in this thesis, providing a methodological reference and practical toolset for the safety evaluation of medical multi-agent systems.
}
